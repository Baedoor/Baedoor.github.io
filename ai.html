Full statement on AI - by Toma400

You can feel safe

I want to start with general disclaimer. If you came here, you most likely want to know in detail what is my approach to genAI - this section is indeed meant to precise all the details.
But what I want to emphasize immediately:

Enjoying my art, you are enjoying purely human art. And this will not change.

You knowing this is important to me, because after genAI became mainstream, I've spent so many hours trying to figure out whether creator I listen, play games of, read, watch, uses genAI in the process.
It is extremely exhausting, and I yearned so much for more creators to be explicit about that. Not even to admit they are against -- though, of course, this is preferrable -- but to set that straight, so that it's not up to guessing game and searching for eerie details.
So here is the statement and a promise I hoped more artists have done. If you are one of such artists, remember that public statement about genAI helps. With normalising the opposition against it, but also with just audience feeling it can feel safe with your art.
You, my dear reader, can feel safe. I may oppose you, frustrate you, challenge you, as all artists do, but if I succeed at achieving any of that, this will be a sword of my own making.

What art means to me

Having most important statement after ourselves, I want to start with quite important piece.

My entire life was, is, and most probably will be until I die, extremely connected to art. I've enjoyed reading comics, books, listening to music and playing games ever since I remember existing. All of that also shaped me, in many ways, but consistently it was one of the biggest teachers - "I wouldn't be the same person without art" sounds almost banal and subtle compared to how much it actually affected me.
From industrialist revolt and critical approach to society, through melancholic love to beauty of goth subculture and music, Tarkovsky movies speaking the language of empathetic spirituality, to the absurd of dadaist art and avant-garde, beauty of the nature of romantic paintings, orientalist art of europeans and actual oriental art of japanese, the pain of heartbreaks found in 80s music, and last but not least my two maestros: a bearded jester Terry Pratchett, and a bearded storyteller Don Rosa. Art is everything to me, and through it I experience the world, relationships, nature, faith.
Through it I also try to speak, as art is for me a language, a communication form that gives us a way to escape the precise, utilitarian, material world. The ambiguity of language, visual and sound is what softens us to the other. And it's a beautiful way we can show ourselves to each other: through poem, book, song, painting. I'm maximalistic in definition of art, so yes, a heartfelt message to someone is, for me, an art in itself - there's not really a big difference between Discord message and a letter, and from letter to a book or poem, if we think about it that way.

Understanding the above is crucial to know from where my position to genAI comes from: it is from place of seeing the intrusion into my most beloved and dear garden, a place which was giving me both peace and a sense of destination in the world. The most stable and everlasting home I could find as a human being.

Have I used AI?

Yes. I think it comes with no surprise to anyone, particularly artists who were there in 2022, when this technology first emerged. Most of anti-AI people of this era tried Midjourney. Most of us tried LLMs. A lot of us had even some innocent fun with it for a while - a journey of critical thought isn't a test of pureness.
As most artists and programmers, I've also wet my feet in that sludge. Initially because I wasn't aware of anything that came with this technology. My rejection came actually pretty quickly: it was even before the famous Steven Zapata's video, and as funny as this sounds, it was thanks to Kotaku's article. Quickly after reading it, I figured out that what I treated as a silly toy (because at that point it was hard to treat Midjourney as anything else) is something rather negative. I haven't thought about it as dangerous or so dramatically awful as I do now, but the first stone started the avalanche that was going to happen.

Have I never touched AI after that? No. I'm a bit of a contrarian being, and while my disgust towards genAI only raised each month and I haven't been convinced at all, I tried a tiny bit of LLMs. Partially, to know what I am rejecting - whether AI for code changes anything to me. Partially, because I felt I'm missing something out (ah, the beautiful fear inflicted upon us by grifters). Partially, because I just like contesting my own beliefs, and I'm curious being.
So yeah, I used AI to assist me when learning Rust language and code my own Discord bot. It was... mixed experience. But most importantly, I noticed how quickly the mind jumped into asking mode before it even tried solving the problem at hand. A familiar approach if we think of today vibecoding, eh?
I quickly realised coding AIs are just a trojan horse under a disguise: in theory, they offer more control over the process than art genAIs. In reality, they teach us the same bad habits, they outsource our thinking just as much, but here also under a disguise of being more productive. And with emergence of vibecoding, programmers are pretty much at the same spot artists were years ago - except they aren't used to protesting, and aren't used to be called luddites. Like a frog cooked in the pot, they accepted their redundancy in the realm of what someone could think once fascinated them: how computers work, and how can you evoke the most complex realities thanks to sheer power of logic and algebra.
So yes, I gave up on LLMs, because I do love coding, I do love learning about the inner workings of computer and it is an art form to me too: giving up on code and structures is like giving up on deciding brushes or layer setup in Krita. Anyone knowing digital art will know what I mean.

After that, I tried LLMs few times for few queries, by the way. Nothing used in any of my projects (and I rewrote the Discord bot to be fully handwritten). Just to see how the technology evolved. But I simply do not find myself interested in that. Whether in typically considered art or code, there's layer of detail and knowledge that would make all activities I loved hollow if I delegated anything to genAI.

Why am I against? Why so radically?
