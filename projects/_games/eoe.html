Emperor of Eternity is game that sit in my mind for years, out of my love to city-builders and the fact that I was making my own strategy games since childhood. This genre in general evoked my imagination almost as heavily as RPGs did.

So, EoE is pretty much bringing this vision to life - taking inspirations from Sierra's "City Builders" series, Stronghold and 0 A.D., it's meant to be a real-time strategy focused on economy and cultural storytelling.
Unlike most games that influenced EoE though, you'd be able to expand over multiple cities, while still retaining their local context as equally important. I'd imagine several smart automation/managing mechanics allowing you to control such increased gameplay scope, so as a player you'd be able to decide to what extent you want to control each detail of your empire.

The core point of the game are civilisations - they would be shown through diverse architecture and variety in infrastructure, as well as unique troops and resource management, including even distinct traditions and religion to care about.
This will let player be immersed and interested in the culture, making it both gameplay feature and the reason to get into each, to learn their advantages and playing style.
Additionally, the game would feature slow progress system - which means the cities and cultures will change over time. Unlike Civilisation or Empire Earth though, the changes would not be drastic and rather support extremely long sandbox plays where you'd be able to see ancient civilisation evolve into the steam era, one evolution at a time.
The game itself will try to allow fully peaceful gameplay, meaning that if you'd prefer to keep yourself out of the combat, the game will support you with wise diplomatic decisions and focus on trade and managing cities.
